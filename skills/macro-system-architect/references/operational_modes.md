# Operational Modes Guide

When analyzing an amorphous problem, first identify the primary Operational Mode. This determines the focus and entry point of the 5-Vector analysis.

---

## Mode A: Problem Discovery (Finding the True Bottleneck)
* **Trigger:** The situation is chaotic, ill-defined, or framed around symptoms rather than root causes ("We don't know what's actually broken," "Everything is falling behind," "We need to fix product velocity").
* **Core Objective:** Strip away narrative noise, corporate optimism, and surface-level complaints to expose the single structural bottleneck that, if resolved, unlocks the rest of the system.
* **Key Analytical Directives:**
  1. Distinguish between proxy metrics (e.g., Jira velocity, meeting frequency) and ground-truth realities (e.g., actual deployable output, defect counts).
  2. Map the path of information to locate where truth gets distorted between reality and decision-makers.
  3. Identify what physical, technical, or resource constraint is being ignored or negotiated against impossibly.

---

## Mode B: Project Organization & Execution Planning
* **Trigger:** A massive, multi-faceted goal or initiative needs to be planned or executed from scratch, or an existing project requires restructuring due to drift ("How do we organize this major initiative?" "How do we build this system from zero?").
* **Core Objective:** Establish vertical functional commands, eliminate administrative latency, enforce absolute technical ownership, and map out a parallelized execution roadmap.
* **Key Analytical Directives:**
  1. Collapse horizontal committee networks into explicit, vertically accountable command lines.
  2. Establish direct physical/operational proximity between designers/architects and execution units (Johnson's 50-foot rule).
  3. Replace sequential development schedules with concurrent, parallel subsystem integration (Von Neumann ICBM model).

---

## Mode C: System Dysfunction Analysis
* **Trigger:** An existing system, team, or operational workflow is actively failing, bogged down in friction, or experiencing severe quality/velocity degradation ("Why is this system/organization failing?" "Why can't we deliver on time?").
* **Core Objective:** Diagnose structural rot, administrative bloat, perverse human capital incentives, and communication breakdowns.
* **Key Analytical Directives:**
  1. Audit team sizing and organizational layers to locate empire-building or administrative self-justification (Johnson's lean team principle).
  2. Audit quality control and feedback loops for "paper reactor" optimism or unmitigated risks (Rickover principle).
  3. Analyze incentive structures to determine if individuals are rewarded for headcount/complexity rather than system output.

---

## Mode D: Multi-System Synthesis
* **Trigger:** Confronting multiple interacting systems, cross-domain dependencies, or macro-level strategic trade-offs ("How do we align these three platforms?" "How do we manage dependencies across these organizations?").
* **Core Objective:** Formulate explicit trade-off matrices, map inter-system cybernetic feedback loops, and establish non-partisan technical alignment nodes.
* **Key Analytical Directives:**
  1. Strip away domain-specific jargon to convert cross-domain interactions into explicit mathematical, logical, or utility matrices (Von Neumann model).
  2. Identify feedback loops where delay or noise in System A creates exponential dysfunction in System B.
  3. Form independent technical advisory nodes to bypass institutional rivalries and establish objective technical consensus.
