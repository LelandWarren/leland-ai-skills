# Warmer

## Overview

A finishing pass over text you've **already drafted** — a review comment, a message, an email, a
ticket — that makes it shorter, warmer, and kinder without losing an ounce of substance or softening
the real verdict. It transforms existing text; it does not generate new content.

**Core principle:** cut words, not facts. Add warmth, not flattery. The result should feel like it
came from a considerate human who respects the reader's time — and still says exactly what it needed
to say.

## When to use

- The user explicitly asks: "make it warmer / shorter / kinder / softer / more human / more
  empathetic", or "tighten this up and make it nicer".
- Always applied to something already written. If there's no draft yet, write it first (with
  whatever content skill fits), then apply this pass last.

## The three moves

**Shorter — trim the words, keep the meaning.**
- Cut throat-clearing ("I just wanted to say", "it's worth noting that"), hedging ("kind of", "I
  think maybe"), and repetition.
- One point, made once, well.
- Never cut a fact, a caveat, or a limitation to save space. Length comes out of *phrasing*, not
  *substance*.

**Warmer — a considerate human, not a form letter.**
- Acknowledge the person and their effort where it's real.
- When there's criticism, anchor it next to what's genuinely good — and mean it.
- A light touch of personality is welcome; an emoji only if the channel and relationship suit it.

**Kinder / empathetic — meet the reader where they are.**
- Read their likely state: stressed, junior, defensive, proud of the work? Adjust accordingly.
- Prefer "here's the one thing" over "here's everything wrong" — lead with what matters most, not an
  exhaustive list.
- Frame so the reader stays in the driver's seat (a suggestion to consider, not a verdict handed
  down) — unless it genuinely is non-negotiable.

## Guardrails (what keeps warm from becoming dishonest)

- **Warmth never changes the verdict.** A blocking issue stays unmistakably blocking; "non-negotiable"
  doesn't get fuzzed into "maybe consider". Kind *and* clear, not kind *instead of* clear.
- **No untrue flattery.** If there's nothing to praise, don't invent it — just be respectful.
- **Don't bury the point to be gentle.** The reader should still get the takeaway on one read.
- **Match the channel and relationship.** A peer MR comment, a Slack ping, a note to someone senior,
  and a customer email each want a different register.

## Composing with other skills

This is a **tone lens** and it runs **last**:

- **Order:** content/clarity skills first (draft the substance, then `writing-plainly` or `eli5` for
  clarity), then this pass for tone. Applying it before the content is settled just means redoing it.
- **It defers to content skills on substance** and never overrides a stated verdict — it changes
  only *how* the settled message lands, not *what* it says.
- **With `unslop`:** both lenses claim the last slot and pull against each other — warmth adds
  the softness `unslop` strips. When both apply, do **one combined pass**; `unslop` treats warmth
  as substance to preserve. Running them in sequence just means each undoes the other.

## Red flags — you've drifted

- A caveat or fact disappeared in the name of "shorter".
- A blocking issue now reads as optional.
- Praise that isn't true, or warmth so thick the point is buried.
- Same length as before — you softened the tone but didn't actually tighten.
- The register is wrong for the channel (gushing on a terse peer note, or cold on a sensitive one).

## Quick check before you send

Is it shorter, does it read as if a thoughtful person wrote it, and does it still deliver every
fact, caveat, and verdict from the draft — with nothing softened that shouldn't be? If yes, send.
