# Unslop

## Overview

A finishing lens that rewrites text so it stops reading as machine-generated. The target is
"a competent human wrote this" — not any particular person's voice. There is no voice profile
and no sample-matching, so it works on anything.

**Core rule: cut the reflexes, keep the facts.**

This is a voice lens, not a detector-evasion tool. It strips machine tells from honest writing.
Do not use it to make attested, graded, or authorship-declared work slip past an AI-detection
check — if someone asks for that, say so plainly and offer the honest version instead.

## When to use

- The user asks directly: make this sound human, de-slop it, less robotic, stop sounding like AI.
- The user asks you to *produce* something and wants it un-AI from the start. Apply the rules
  while drafting rather than writing slop and scrubbing it after.

Explicit invocation only. Do not apply this uninvited.

## The density principle — read this before anything else

Almost every tell below is also a legitimate move a good writer makes sometimes. Em dashes are
real punctuation. A three-part list is a real device. A one-line fragment can land hard.

What gives AI away is not that these appear. It is that they appear *on reflex*, at a rate no
human sustains.

So work by density, not prohibition. Do not ban the em dash. Notice six of them in four
paragraphs and keep one. A skill that outlaws its tell list produces prose that reads as if it
is dodging something, which is its own tell.

## The tell catalogue

### Fake rhetoric

- **Negative parallelism.** "It's not just a migration — it's a fundamental shift." Manufactured
  contrast used to fake depth. Also the staccato version: "Not ten. Not fifty. Five hundred."
- **Reflex rule of three.** "scalable, efficient, and robust." Triplets everywhere, including
  where two or four is the honest count.
- **Question then snap answer.** "The result? Game-changing." "And the best part? It's free."
- **Dramatic fragments** split onto their own line for emphasis that was never earned.

### Word tells

- **Nouns and adjectives:** delve, tapestry, realm, landscape, testament, beacon, ecosystem,
  synergy, vibrant, multifaceted, pivotal, seamless, robust, holistic, nuanced.
- **Verbs:** leverage, underscore, foster, unlock, navigate, elevate, illuminate, harness, embark.
- **Inflated copulas:** "serves as", "stands as", "represents", "marks", "reflects" — where "is"
  or "shows" was right there.
- **Stock openers:** "In today's fast-paced digital world", "In the realm of", "At its core",
  "Let's explore", "It's important to note", "That being said".
- **Transition crutches** starting sentences: furthermore, moreover, additionally, consequently,
  crucially.

### Shape tells

- **The sandwich.** Restate the prompt, then 3–5 bullets of near-identical length and grammar,
  then a tidy "In summary" wrap-up.
- **Bold-colon bullets** where every bullet has the same **Stem:** explanation shape.
- **Metronome rhythm.** Every sentence between 12 and 20 words.
- **Uniform blocks.** Every paragraph 3–4 sentences.
- **Preamble counting.** "Here are three key considerations to keep in mind."
- **Over-formatting.** Headers and bullets on something a human would answer in two messy
  sentences, like a Slack reply.

### Stance tells

- **Fence-sitting.** "While X offers clear benefits, Y presents challenges, making a balanced
  approach essential." Says nothing, at length.
- **Reflex hedging.** arguably, generally speaking, tends to, to some extent, it could be argued.
- **Stakes inflation.** A small refactor described as a testament to operational excellence.
- **Recap conclusions** that repeat the body instead of landing somewhere.
- **Frictionless polish.** No quirks, no sharp take, no specifics, no mess.

## The moves

Work in this order.

**1. Delete the reflexes.** Throat-clearing opener. "In summary" wrap-up. Preamble counting.
Sentence-initial transition crutches. Most of these can go with nothing put back.

**2. Deflate.** "serves as" becomes "is". "underscores" becomes "shows". Word tells become the
plain word that was always underneath.

**3. Break the symmetry.** Cut a triplet to two, or push it to four when four is true. Vary
sentence length deliberately — a five-word sentence next to a long winding one. Let paragraphs
run uneven.

**4. Take the stance.** Turn "while X, Y must also be considered" into whichever one is actually
meant. Keep hedging only where the uncertainty is real. Real uncertainty stated plainly is not
a tell.

**5. Thin the density.** Count the em dashes, bolded stems, and fragments that survived. Keep
the ones that earn their place.

## Guardrails

- **Never invent a fact.** No made-up numbers, names, dates, examples, or anecdotes. The
  "frictionless polish" tell tempts you to add colour. Adding colour to factual writing is how
  a de-slop pass turns a correct MR description into a wrong one. Where the prose is begging
  for a concrete specific it does not have, leave `[need a real example here]` and move on.
- **Never break a required skeleton.** MR description sections, doc headers, mandated bullet
  structures stay exactly as they are. Attack the sentences inside them. If the format demands
  bullets, the bullets survive.
- **Never change the verdict.** Sharpening a hedge means saying what was meant, not saying
  something different. A blocking issue stays blocking.
- **Do not overcorrect.** Prose written to avoid a list of words has just traded one tell for
  another.
- **Keep it plain.** Removing AI polish does not mean adding ornament. The fix for an inflated
  verb is a simple one, never a fancier one.

## Composing with other skills

This is a tone lens and it runs at the end.

Order: content skill first, then `writing-plainly` or `grade6` for clarity, then this.

`warmer` also claims the last slot, and the two pull against each other — warmth adds
acknowledgment and softness, which is the smooth register this lens strips. **When both apply,
do one combined pass.** Treat warmth as substance to preserve, the same way you preserve facts.
Running them one after the other just means each undoes the other.

## Red flags — you've drifted

- A fact, number, or caveat changed or vanished.
- You invented an example to make a paragraph feel lived-in.
- A required section or bullet list got flattened into prose.
- The output reads as though it is avoiding certain words.
- Every trace of structure is gone and the thing is now harder to scan.
- It is the same length with fancier synonyms. You swapped words instead of cutting reflexes.

## Quick check before you hand it back

Read it aloud in your head. Does the rhythm vary? Does it commit to something? Is every fact
from the draft still there, unchanged? Could a careful person have typed this?
